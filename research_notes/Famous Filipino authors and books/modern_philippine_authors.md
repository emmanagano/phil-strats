# Modern and Contemporary Books by Philippine-Based Filipino Authors (c. 1960–2026)

Research date: 2026-11-18. About 17 searches/fetches. Many sales figures are old (2012–2014) press reports, not audited publisher data. Where the only source was a Wikipedia page or a mirror of one, that is noted.

## Literary giants and their signature books: which are most widely read/known and why?

### Takeaway
The canon most Filipino readers meet in school and on film is anchored by Nick Joaquin, F. Sionil José (Rosales Saga), Lualhati Bautista (Dekada '70; Bata, Bata... Pa'no Ka Ginawa?), Amado V. Hernandez (Mga Ibong Mandaragit) and Edgardo M. Reyes (Sa mga Kuko ng Liwanag). The Tagalog novels reached mass audiences largely through landmark film adaptations, while Joaquin and José also gained international reach through Penguin Classics and translations.

### Cited Findings

**Nick Joaquin (1917–2004), English. National Artist for Literature 1976**
- National Artist for Literature, 1976 — [Wikipedia: National Artist of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Artist_of_the_Philippines)
- *The Woman Who Had Two Navels*: novel first published by Regal Publishing in 1961; it won the Stonehill Award for the Filipino novel in English — [Philstar](https://philstar.com/lifestyle/arts-and-culture/2017/05/01/1689889/nick-joaquins-tropical-gothic-tales-now-penguin-classics); [ABS-CBN](https://abs-cbn.com/life/04/28/17/penguin-classics-honors-nick-joaquin-with-new-collection)
- Penguin Classics published *The Woman Who Had Two Navels and Tales of the Tropical Gothic* (2017, ed. Vicente Rafael) for his birth centenary. It includes "May Day Eve", "The Summer Solstice" and the play *A Portrait of the Artist as Filipino*. Joaquin was the third Filipino in Penguin Classics, after José Rizal and José Garcia Villa — [ABS-CBN](https://abs-cbn.com/life/04/28/17/penguin-classics-honors-nick-joaquin-with-new-collection); [Penguin Random House](https://randomhousebooks.com/books/549468); [NPR review](https://www.npr.org/2017/04/19/523586256/a-potent-uneasy-blend-of-passion-and-fatalism-in-the-woman-who-had-two-navels)
- Died April 29, 2004 — [nickjoaquin.blogspot (secondary)](https://nickjoaquin.blogspot.com/2005/05/nick-joaquin-and-his-contemporaries.html)

**F. Sionil José (1924–2022), English. National Artist 2001, Ramon Magsaysay Award 1980**
- The Rosales Saga is five novels set in his hometown of Rosales, Pangasinan: *The Pretenders*, *Tree*, *My Brother, My Executioner*, *Mass* and *Po-on* (the latter later published as *Dusk*, per common usage; the alternate title is not confirmed in the sources fetched). It covers about 100 years of Philippine history (1872–1972) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._Sionil_Jos%C3%A9); [ABS-CBN](https://news.abs-cbn.com/life/01/06/22/f-sionil-jose-dies-at-97)
- National Artist for Literature, 2001 — [Wikipedia: National Artist list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Artist_of_the_Philippines). Ramon Magsaysay Award, 1980 (journalism, literature) — [RMAF tribute](https://rmaward.asia/tribute-for-francisco-sionil-jose/)
- Often described as the leading Filipino candidate for the Nobel Prize in Literature — [Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/?p=1537637); [CCP](https://culturalcenter.gov.ph/press-release/ccp-mourns-national-artist-f-sionil-jose/)
- **Died January 6, 2022**, aged 97, at Makati Medical Center (the day before a scheduled angioplasty). Buried at the Libingan ng mga Bayani on January 18, 2022 — [ABS-CBN](https://news.abs-cbn.com/life/01/06/22/f-sionil-jose-dies-at-97); [Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2022/1/17/national-artist-f-sionil-jose-to-be-laid-to-rest-at-libingan-ng-mga-bayani)

**Lualhati Bautista (1945–2023), Tagalog/Filipino**
- Palanca grand prizes for the novel: *Gapô* (1980), *Dekada '70* (1983) and *Bata, Bata… Pa'no Ka Ginawa?* (1984). She was known for writing about abuse and about life under Marcos Sr.'s martial law — [Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/headlines/2023/02/12/2244433/writer-activist-lualhati-bautista-dies-77); [Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/?p=1728900)
- *Dekada '70* film (2002), dir. Chito S. Roño, Star Cinema, starring Vilma Santos, Christopher de Leon and Piolo Pascual. It won Best Picture at the 28th Metro Manila Film Festival, plus acting awards — [Wikipedia: Dekada '70 (film)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dekada_%2770_(film)); [Philstar 2002 review](https://www.philstar.com/entertainment/2002/12/14/187788/dekada-14670-not-great-film-important-one/amp/)
- Vilma Santos starred in both film adaptations (*Dekada '70* and *Bata, Bata*) — [Inquirer Entertainment](https://entertainment.inquirer.net/485672/vilma-santos-carlo-aquino-react-to-lualhati-bautistas-death)
- In January 2022, Penguin Classics editor Elda Rotor approached her to publish *Dekada '70* as a Penguin Classic. A self-published English translation, *The '70s* (trans. Clarisse B. de Jesus), came out in 2021 — [Rappler](https://www.rappler.com/life-and-style/literature/lualhati-bautista-gets-offer-penguin-publish-dekada-70/); [Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2022/1/29/lualhati-bautistas-dekada-70-novel-to-become-a-penguin-classics-edition); [Wikipedia: Dekada '70 (novel)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dekada_%2770_(novel))
- **Died February 2023, aged 77**, at home in Quezon City. Sources disagree on the date: most say February 12, while Philstar's first report said 6 a.m. on February 11 — [Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/headlines/2023/02/12/2244433/writer-activist-lualhati-bautista-dies-77); [ABS-CBN](https://news.abs-cbn.com/life/02/13/23/vilma-santos-pays-tribute-to-lualhati-bautista); [GMA](https://www.gmanetwork.com/entertainment/showbiznews/news/98048/lualhati-bautista-passes-away-at-77/story)

**Amado V. Hernandez (1903–1970), Tagalog. National Artist 1973 (first batch)**
- *Mga Ibong Mandaragit* (1969), a "nobelang sosyo-politikal" about the struggle for justice during and after the Japanese occupation. He wrote it while imprisoned — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Mga_Ibong_Mandaragit); [mgaibongmandaragit site](https://sites.google.com/view/mgaibongmandaragit/home)
- Penguin (SEA) published an English translation, *The Birds of Prey* — [Penguin SG author page](https://www.penguin.sg/book_author/amado-v-hernandez/)
- National Artist for Literature, 1973 — [Wikipedia: National Artist list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Artist_of_the_Philippines)

**Edgardo M. Reyes (Tagalog)**
- *Sa mga Kuko ng Liwanag* was serialized in *Liwayway* (1966–67) and published as a book in 1986 by De La Salle University Press. A fisherman, Julio, goes to Manila to search for his betrothed, Ligaya. Lino Brocka adapted it as the film *Maynila: Sa mga Kuko ng Liwanag* — [Wikipedia: In the Claws of Brightness](https://www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_Claws_of_Brightness)

**N.V.M. Gonzalez (1915–1999), English. National Artist 1997**
- National Artist 1997 — [Wikipedia: National Artist list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Artist_of_the_Philippines). His major works include *The Bamboo Dancers* (1959, just before this period). Died 1999 — [Wikipedia](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/NVM_Gonzalez)

**Edith L. Tiempo (1919–2011), English. National Artist 1999**
- Poet, fictionist and teacher, and co-founder of the Silliman writers' tradition in Dumaguete. National Artist 1999. Died August 21, 2011, in Dumaguete — [Wikipedia](https://Www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edith_Tiempo); [Ateneo ALIWW](https://rizaldb.ateneo.edu/aliww/english_etiempo.html)

**Bienvenido Lumbera (1932–2021), Filipino/English. Poet, critic, librettist**
- Ramon Magsaysay Award 1993 (journalism, literature and creative communication arts); National Artist for Literature 2006. **Died September 28, 2021**, aged 89, of stroke complications — [Philstar](https://philstar.com/headlines/2021/09/28/2130310/national-artist-literature-bienvenido-lumbera-89); [Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2021/9/28/national-artist-for-literature-bienvenido-lumbera-passes-away); one Wikipedia line gives 1996 for the Magsaysay, which conflicts with the other sources — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bienvenido_Lumbera)

**Jessica Zafra, English essays/fiction**
- *Twisted* (1995) collected her newspaper column of the same name (in *Today*). The series ran to at least nine books (1995–2011), and a 25th-anniversary edition came out in October 2021 — [Zafra's blog](https://www.jessicarulestheuniverse.com/twisted/announcements); [Goodreads author blog](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/21825019-twisted-the-25th-anniversary-edition-is-now-available-on-shopee?tab=author)

### Inferences
- **Reach versus acclaim.** The Tagalog novels (Bautista, Reyes, Hernandez) are probably the most widely known to ordinary Filipinos. Film made them famous: Brocka's *Maynila*, Roño's *Dekada '70*, and Vilma Santos in both Bautista films. Joaquin and José are more prestige and canon figures, with more international reach (Penguin Classics; Nobel talk).
- **Martial law.** It is the unifying theme of the most famous Filipino-language novels of the period (*Dekada '70*, *Mga Ibong Mandaragit*'s social critique, *Sa mga Kuko ng Liwanag*'s urban poverty). That helps explain their lasting place in classrooms and in political discourse.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source was fetched for DepEd/CHED curriculum inclusion of these titles. They are widely taught (e.g. *Dekada '70*, *Sa mga Kuko ng Liwanag* in Filipino classes), but that is unverified here.
- Release year of Brocka's *Maynila* (generally 1975) and of the *Bata, Bata* film (generally 1998, Star Cinema) were not confirmed by the sources retrieved.
- Edgardo M. Reyes's death date (believed 2012) was not verified.
- No sales figures were found for any literary classic.
- **Ninotchka Rosca** (*State of War*, 1988) is primarily a US-based diaspora author and is left to the diaspora researcher. **Gregorio Brillantes** is covered under the National Artists question below.
- The *Po-on* = *Dusk* title equivalence (Random House US edition) is commonly stated but was not explicitly confirmed in the fetched sources.

## Popular and bestselling authors: Bob Ong, Ricky Lee, komiks, Wattpad

### Takeaway
Mass-market popularity in Filipino publishing belongs to Bob Ong (Visprint), Ricky Lee's novels, and the Wattpad-to-print-to-film pipeline (*She's Dating the Gangster*, *Diary ng Panget*, *He's Into Her*). By local standards, where a literary title may struggle to sell 1,000 copies a year, tens of thousands of copies counts as a blockbuster.

### Cited Findings

**Bob Ong (pseudonymous; Filipino/Taglish humor)**
- *ABNKKBSNPLAko?!* (2001, Visprint) is his first and most popular book. Its first run of 500 copies sold out quickly. About 240,000 copies had been printed and sold by 2012–2013, making it Visprint's bestselling title — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABNKKBSNPLAko%3F!); [Streetprint Manila](https://manila.streetprint.org/items/15813)
- Six of his books have reportedly each passed a quarter of a million copies (publisher/bookseller copy, not audited) — [NewSouth Books](https://newsouthbooks.com.au/books/the-boy-with-a-snake-in-his-schoolbag-a-memoir-from-manila-or-something-like-that/)
- Viva Films adapted it as a film, released February 19, 2014 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABNKKBSNPLAko%3F!)
- An English translation was published internationally as *The Boy with a Snake in His Schoolbag: A Memoir from Manila (Or Something Like That)* (Tuttle, trans. Freely Abrigo) — [WHSmith](https://www.whsmith.co.uk/products/the-boy-with-a-snake-in-his-schoolbag-a-memoir-from-manila-or-something-like-that/bob-ong/freely-abrigo/hardback/9780804855211.html)

**Ricky Lee (b. 1948), Filipino. Novelist and the country's most-awarded screenwriter**
- *Para kay B (o kung paano dinevastate ng pag-ibig ang 4 out of 5 sa atin)* (2008) sold about 30,000 copies in two years. *Si Amapola sa 65 na Kabanata* (2011), with a manananggal heroine, sold about 10,000 copies within days of release. Literary authors, by comparison, "find it difficult to dispose of the first print order of 1,000 copies in a year" — [Philstar, Jan 2012](https://philstar.com/entertainment/2012/01/27/771382/building-audience-books-movies)
- Sequel *Lahat ng B* (2022). An English edition of *Para kay B* was published by Ateneo University Press as *For B* — [GMA](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/lifestyle/artandculture/865623/ricky-lee-on-para-kay-b-its-sequel-lahat-ng-b-and-keeping-his-promise-of-writing-personal-dedicat/story); [Ateneo Press](https://unipress.ateneo.edu/node/4254); [PhilstarLife](https://star35.philstarlife.com/geeky/228794-ricky-lee-para-kay-b-english-version)
- National Artist for **Film and Broadcast Arts** (not Literature), 2022 — [NCCA](https://ncca.gov.ph/?p=26371); [Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/lifestyle/2022/06/30/2192041/ricky-lee-snubbed-himala-dedicates-national-artist-award-fellow-suppressed-writers). One Inquirer piece wrongly says Literature — [Inquirer](https://entertainment.inquirer.net/?p=453095)

**Wattpad phenomena**
- The Philippines is Wattpad's second-largest market after the US (about 7 million monthly users at the time of the report). Per Wattpad, it has adapted more Wattpad stories into print, TV and film than any other country. The TV5 *Wattpad Presents* series (from 2014) produced 250 episodes from 76 stories — [BusinessWorld](https://www.bworldonline.com/?p=154593); [Wikipedia: Wattpad](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Wattpad)
- *She's Dating the Gangster*, by Bianca Bernardino (posted 2006): the Summit Books print edition (March 2013) topped National Book Store's Philippine bestseller list and sold over 50,000 copies. The 2014 Star Cinema film (dir. Cathy Garcia-Molina; Kathryn Bernardo and Daniel Padilla) grossed a reported ₱296 million (aggregator figure) — [Vera Files](https://verafiles.org/articles/book-power-perks-up-the-movies); [Rappler](https://www.rappler.com/entertainment/49246-kathniel-shes-dating-the-gangster); [Wikipedia](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/She%27s_Dating_the_Gangster)
- *Diary ng Panget*, by HaveYouSeenThisGirL (Denny R.): over 12 million reads, and the first three books sold 140,000 copies combined. The April 2014 Viva Films adaptation (dir. Andoy Ranay; Nadine Lustre and James Reid) grossed about ₱120 million (Wikipedia-derived) — [Vera Files](https://verafiles.org/articles/book-power-perks-up-the-movies); [Rappler interview](https://www.rappler.com/entertainment/54191-interview-denny-author-diary-ng-panget); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diary_ng_Panget)
- Sources disagree on which was the "first Wattpad film": *Diary ng Panget* or *She's Dating the Gangster* (both 2014) — [Wikipedia list of Wattpad films](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/List_of_Philippine_films_based_on_Wattpad_stories)
- *He's Into Her*, by Maxinejiji (Maxine Lat Calibuso), was adapted as a 2021 iWantTFC series starring Belle Mariano and Donny Pangilinan. Viva One revived the trend with Gwy Saludes' University Series. In 2026, Viva has *Project Loki*, *My Husband Is a Mafia Boss* and *Hell University* in the pipeline. Viva and WEBTOON Productions signed a three-year first-look deal in 2025 — [Daily Tribune, Feb 2026](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/02/06/the-complicated-comeback-of-wattpad-adaptations); [LionhearTV](https://www.lionheartv.net/2025/08/viva-communications-inc-and-webtoon-productions-seal-first-look-development-and-production-deal-to-bring-more-hit-stories-to-viva-one)
- GMA Network and Wattpad partnered on TV adaptations under the "Luv Is" banner (2022) — [Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2022/6/17/gma-network-and-wattpad-join-forces-for-tv-adaptations-of-hit-webnovels-via-luv-is)

### Inferences
- Bob Ong is very likely the single bestselling Filipino-language author of the 2000s. His roughly 240,000-copy debut dwarfs any literary-novel figure found.
- Wattpad stories are the main mass-market fiction engine for Filipino youth since about 2013. Their fame is measured in reads and box office rather than literary prizes.
- Ricky Lee bridges literary acclaim and mass popularity: he is a National Artist (Film) whose novels sell like popular fiction.

### Gaps
- No sources were retrieved on **Mars Ravelo** (*Darna*, *Dyesebel*, etc.). His komiks work is mostly pre-1960s serials, endlessly adapted for film and TV, and arguably out of scope as "books".
- No current (post-2014) read counts for *She's Dating the Gangster*, *Diary ng Panget*, *He's Into Her* or *Hell University* were found.
- No verified sales or details were found for Lee's *Trip to Quiapo* (scriptwriting manual, 1998), which is widely called a perennial bestseller.
- Other popular names not researched here: Eros Atalia (*Taguan-Pung*, *Ligo na U, Lapit na Me*, adapted to film 2011), Mina V. Esguerra (romance), and Psicom horror/Wattpad imprints.

## Recent (2015–2026) Filipino books with notable buzz, awards or adaptations

### Takeaway
The National Book Awards for 2023–2024 went to historical and literary novels from university presses (Ateneo, UP). Mass buzz has come from new Viva One and GMA Wattpad adaptations and from Penguin's interest in Filipino classics.

### Cited Findings
- **41st National Book Awards** (announced Dec 2023, ceremony Feb 2024): Best Novel in English, *Yñiga* by Glenn Diaz; Best Novel in Filipino, *El Arbol de Alegria: Southern Quartet I* by RM Topacio-Aplaon; Best Novel in Binisaya, *Arkipelago: Novela* by Januar Yap (Advaux) — [Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1873152/nbdb-national-book-awards-winners); [UP](https://up.edu.ph/up-faculty-and-alumni-up-press-win-41st-national-book-awards/)
- **42nd National Book Awards** (Nov 2024): Best Novel in English, *1762: A Novel* by Vin dela Serna Lopez (Ateneo Press); Best Novel in Filipino, *Teorya ng Unang Panahon* by Edgar Calabia Samar. Ateneo and UP Press were joint Publishers of the Year — [PhilstarLife](https://philstarlife.com/news-and-views/711614-42nd-national-book-awards-winners); [BusinessWorld](https://www.bworldonline.com/arts-and-leisure/2024/11/27/637492/ateneo-press-up-press-named-publishers-of-the-year); [Ateneo](https://cms-training.ateneo.edu/features/2024/11/08/ateneo-university-press-takes-home-7-wins-42nd-national-book-awards)
- **43rd National Book Awards** finalists were announced in December 2025 — [The Post](https://thepost.net.ph/news/nation/nbdb-unveils-finalists-for-43rd-national-book-awards/)
- Ricky Lee's *Lahat ng B* (2022) is the long-awaited *Para kay B* sequel — [Inquirer](https://lifestyle.inquirer.net/?p=73173); [GMA](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/lifestyle/artandculture/865623/ricky-lee-on-para-kay-b-its-sequel-lahat-ng-b-and-keeping-his-promise-of-writing-personal-dedicat/story)
- Wattpad adaptations remained active in 2021–2026: *He's Into Her* (2021) and the Viva One University Series, with *Hell University* and others announced — [Daily Tribune](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/02/06/the-complicated-comeback-of-wattpad-adaptations)
- Edgar Calabia Samar is also known for the *Janus Silang* YA fantasy series (not verified in sources fetched; see Gaps)

### Inferences
- The recent awarded literary novels are critically recognized but lack evidence of mass sales. For the report, "notable recent buzz" in the popular sense is better represented by Wattpad adaptations and by Ricky Lee.

### Gaps
- 43rd National Book Awards winners (2025/2026 cycle) were not found.
- No recent Palanca grand-prize novel winners were retrieved.
- No reliable 2015–2026 bestseller lists (e.g. National Book Store or Fully Booked charts) were found.
- *Janus Silang* (Samar; Adarna House, 2014 onward) and its adaptation status are unverified.

## Which authors were named National Artists for Literature (with years)?

### Takeaway
Eighteen writers have been named National Artist for Literature from 1973 to 2026. The most recent are Gémino Abad (2022), and Nicanor Tiongson and Gregorio Brillantes (posthumous), proclaimed in September 2026 as part of a batch of 10 (some outlets call it the "2025" batch).

### Cited Findings
- Roster with years, per [Wikipedia: National Artist of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Artist_of_the_Philippines) (years are inferred from batch groupings):
  - 1973: Amado V. Hernández; José García Villa
  - 1976: Nick Joaquin
  - 1982: Carlos P. Romulo
  - 1990: Francisco Arcellana
  - 1997: N.V.M. Gonzalez (Carlos Quirino, also 1997, is listed under "Historical Literature")
  - 1999: Edith L. Tiempo
  - 2001: F. Sionil José
  - 2003: Virgilio S. Almario; Alejandro R. Roces
  - 2006: Bienvenido Lumbera
  - 2009: Lázaro Francisco (proclamation; conferment delayed to 2013)
  - 2014: Cirilo F. Bautista
  - 2018: Ramon Muzones (posthumous; Hiligaynon novelist); Resil Mojares
  - 2022: Gémino H. Abad
  - 2026: Nicanor Tiongson; Gregorio C. Brillantes (posthumous)
- 2018 Literature awardees Mojares and Muzones are confirmed by [Alberta Filipino Journal](https://www.albertafilipinojournal.com/?p=4502)
- The 2026 batch came under Proclamation No. 1414, s. 2026, announced September 9, 2026: 10 National Artists, including Tiongson (critic, dramatist, former CCP artistic director) and Brillantes (English short fiction, Palanca Hall of Fame; died 2025, with one source giving September 26, 2025). PhilstarLife dates the proclamation to September 1, and some outlets label the group "2025 National Artists" — [Daily Tribune](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/09/10/ten-filipinos-named-national-artists-in-largest-batch-yet-including-first-woman-in-visual-arts-2); [GMA](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/lifestyle/artandculture/1001806/10-new-national-artists-of-the-philippines-named/story/); [Tribune on Brillantes](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/09/09/gregorio-c-brillantes-from-camiling-to-andromeda); [Tribune on Tiongson](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/09/09/nicanor-tiongson-champion-of-philippine-culture); [Context.ph](https://context.ph/2026/09/13/ten-filipinos-named-national-artists-for-2025/)
- Ricky Lee (2022) is a National Artist for Film and Broadcast Arts, not Literature — [NCCA](https://ncca.gov.ph/?p=26371)
- Recent deaths of National Artists for Literature: F. Sionil José (Jan 6, 2022), Bienvenido Lumbera (Sept 28, 2021), Gregorio Brillantes (2025) — sources above. The Wikipedia roster also lists a 2026 death year for Resil Mojares — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Artist_of_the_Philippines) (**unverified by any news source**; treat with caution)

### Inferences
- Of the National Artists for Literature, the authors with books that are actually widely read (rather than critically honored) are Joaquin, José, Hernandez and, to a lesser extent, Gonzalez and Tiempo. Almario, Lumbera, Mojares, Abad and Tiongson are honored mainly as poets, critics, scholars or historians.
- Lualhati Bautista and Edgardo M. Reyes, arguably the most widely read Tagalog novelists of the period, were never named National Artists.

### Gaps
- The year attributions come from a Wikipedia table, cross-checked only partly (1973, 1976, 1999, 2001, 2006, 2018, 2022, 2026). The NCCA's official list was not fetched.
- Resil Mojares's reported 2026 death needs confirmation.
- Virgilio Almario's and Cirilo Bautista's signature books were not researched.
