## 2. Candidate books not yet on the site (4–6) and Open Library IDs

### Takeaway
The strongest new add is **F. Sionil José's *Ermita* (1988)**. It is set in the Manila district the Intramuros guide already recommends as a place to stay (`stay: Ermita / Malate`), so it supports `setIn: intramuros-manila`, and it has an Open Library ID and cover. Three more classics are well sourced: **Linda Ty-Casper's *The Peninsulars* (1964)**, **Kerima Polotan's *The Hand of the Enemy* (1962; 1961 Stonehill Award)** and **Jessica Hagedorn's *Dream Jungle* (2003)**. Separately, **the five late-November candidates now have Open Library IDs**, so they are no longer blocked.

### Cited Findings

**1. *Ermita: A Filipino Novel*, F. Sionil José (1988). Novel, English. setIn: `intramuros-manila` (Ermita/Malate stay area). Open Library: `OL444934W` (cover_i 12968058, check by eye)**
- Published in Manila in 1988 by Solidaridad Publishing House, José's own press, 323 pages — [Filipinas Heritage Library](https://www.filipinaslibrary.org.ph/biblio/60957); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ermita_(novel))
- Set in Ermita, Manila. It moves through prewar, postwar and pre-martial-law Ermita, once the district of Manila's elite. Sources give different main periods, since the novel spans several — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ermita_(novel)); [TripFiction](https://www.tripfiction.com/books/ermita-filipino-novel/)
- A UP *Philippine Humanities Review* article says the novel plays on Ermita's red-light reputation, but the title character's story pushes against the expectation that it is "about" prostitution — [UP journals (PDF)](https://journals.upd.edu.ph/index.php/phr/article/download/4659/4203) (snippet)
- José's Solidaridad Bookshop was on Padre Faura Street in Ermita, which is a real stop for literary travelers — [Wikipedia: F. Sionil José](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/F.Sionil_Jose) (snippet)
- José is already on the shelf (*Po-on*, *The Pretenders*). This would be his third book.

**2. *The Peninsulars*, Linda Ty-Casper (1964). Historical novel, English. setIn: possibly `intramuros-manila` (unconfirmed). Open Library: `OL2611470W` (no cover; typographic)**
- Published in Manila in 1964 (Bookmark), 303 pp. The library catalog classifies it under Philippine history fiction, 1521–1812 — [Ortigas Foundation Library](https://www.ortigasfoundationlibrary.com.ph/collections/library-holdings/peninsulars-novel); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Ty_Casper)
- Her debut novel, set in the Spanish colonial era (18th century), and credited with setting a precedent for using historical fact in fiction — [Inquirer Lifestyle](https://lifestyle.inquirer.net/?p=108737) (snippet); [Inquirer Plus](https://plus.inquirer.net/lifestyle/a-family-torn-by-revolution/) (snippet)
- Literary historian Edna Zapanta Manlapaz called its publication a landmark that claimed literature's role in interpreting history, and noted a woman's entry into a traditionally male genre — [Ateneo ALIWW](https://rizaldb.ateneo.edu/aliww/english_ltcasper.html) (snippet)
- First of a loose trilogy with *The Three-Cornered Sun* (1979) and *The Stranded Whale* (2002) — [Inquirer Plus](https://plus.inquirer.net/lifestyle/a-family-torn-by-revolution/) (snippet); [Quezon.ph foreword](https://www.quezon.ph/2024/03/16/foreword-to-the-three-cornered-sun/)
- Do **not** call it "the first Filipino historical novel in English". No source supports that.

**3. *The Hand of the Enemy*, Kerima Polotan (1962). Novel, English. No setIn. Open Library: `OL2330201W` (cover_i 13845002, check by eye)**
- Won the 1961 Stonehill Award. The Umpil directory, cited in her obituary, lists it as published in 1962 — [Philstar obituary, Aug 21, 2011](https://www.philstar.com/headlines/2011/08/21/718630/writer-and-journalist-kerima-polotan-tuvera-85); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerima_Polotan_Tuvera)
- Polotan won four Palanca first prizes and Free Press short-story prizes. She died in August 2011 at 85 — [Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/headlines/2011/08/21/718630/writer-and-journalist-kerima-polotan-tuvera-85)
- It fills a gap: a mid-century woman novelist from the "modern" era. Keep the authorNote to her writing. She was married to Marcos-era official Juan Tuvera, and that history needs neutral handling or leaving out.
- Its setting was not confirmed in any source I read.

**4. *Dream Jungle*, Jessica Hagedorn (2003). Novel, English. No confirmed setIn. Open Library: `OL3254082W` (cover_i 402324)**
- Published in 2003 by Viking (Penguin) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dream_Jungle); [Penguin Random House](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/288224/dream-jungle-by-jessica-hagedorn/9780142001097/)
- Set in the 1970s Philippines. It draws on two real events: the 1971 "discovery" of the Tasaday as a supposed Stone Age tribe, and the 1976–77 filming of *Apocalypse Now* — [NPR, Oct 19, 2003](https://www.npr.org/2003/10/19/1471271/dream-jungle-of-the-philippines); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dream_Jungle)
- Mixed reception. PW called it barbed and alluring, Kirkus called it ambitious but disjointed, and Penguin quotes the *NYT Book Review* calling it her best since *Dogeaters* — [Publishers Weekly](https://publishersweekly.com/9780670884582); [Kirkus](https://www.kirkusreviews.com/book-reviews/jessica-hagedorn/dream-jungle/print); [Penguin Random House](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/288224/dream-jungle-by-jessica-hagedorn/9780142001097/)
- Hagedorn is already on the shelf (*Dogeaters*).

**5. (Weaker) *Killing Time in a Warm Place*, Jose Dalisay Jr. (1992). Novel, English. Open Library: `OL3891246W` (1992 edition, cover_i 12752911)**
- Open Library record only. I did not source a "why it matters" for it in this pass, so it needs sources before use. Dalisay is already on the shelf (*Soledad's Sister*).

**6. (Watch, not add) *Between You a River*, Kimberly Garza (Holt, Dec 1, 2026)** — see section 1. No Open Library record yet, and too new to count as "well-known".
