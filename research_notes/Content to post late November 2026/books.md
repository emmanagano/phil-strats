## 1. Candidate books not yet on the site (6–10, with setIn where supported)

### Takeaway
Ten candidates fill clear gaps: Ilocano, Ifugao and Hiligaynon works, Tagalog poetry, a children's classic, a play, and a 2026 prizewinner. Only two have a solid `setIn` match: Joaquin's *A Portrait of the Artist as Filipino* → `intramuros-manila`, and the Hudhud → `banaue-rice-terraces-ifugao`. One more is likely but unconfirmed: *Banana Heart Summer* → `albay-mayon-volcano`. The strongest overall adds are *Portrait*, *Banana Heart Summer*, *Lola Basyang*, *Margosatubig* and *Isabela*.

### Cited Findings

**1. *A Portrait of the Artist as Filipino*, Nick Joaquin (written 1950; first published 1952). Play, English. setIn: `intramuros-manila`**
- Literary play in English, written in 1950. It is described as Joaquin's "most popular play". It was first published in 1952 (in a women's magazine and an anthology), aired on radio, and then staged in 1955 — [Wikipedia (snippet)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Portrait_of_the_Artist_as_Filipino)
- Setting: Intramuros in October 1941, almost entirely inside the Marasigan house on Calle Real. The title refers to a self-portrait by the painter Don Lorenzo Marasigan — [Wikipedia (snippet)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Portrait_of_the_Artist_as_Filipino); [Philstar, "Intramuros of broken dreams"](https://www.philstar.com/sunday-life/2013/05/26/946217/intramuros-broken-dreams)
- Staged in the 1950s in Intramuros, the place where it is set — [Philstar column](https://philstar.com/opinion/2009/02/14/439875/elegy-intramuroseulogy-loved-one)
- Adapted as the musical film *Larawan* for the 2017 Metro Manila Film Festival (libretto by Rolando Tinio) — [Inquirer Lifestyle](https://lifestyle.inquirer.net/?p=280863)
- Suggested summary (original): In the last months before the Pacific War, two unmarried sisters keep their family's old house in Intramuros going, along with their father's final painting. Relatives, buyers and old friends push them to sell both.
- Conflict: stage date is 1955 per Wikipedia, while Philstar says only "the 1950s".

**2. *Banana Heart Summer*, Merlinda Bobis (2005). Novel, English. Possible setIn: `albay-mayon-volcano` (unconfirmed)**
- Coming-of-age novel narrated by a girl on Remedios Street in Bicol. Each chapter is built around a local dish — search summaries of [Diliman Review, "The Heart of the Nation"](https://www.journals.upd.edu.ph/index.php/dilimanreview/article/view/3540) and [Reading Group Guides](https://admin.readinggroupguides.com/reviews/banana-heart-summer)
- Won the Gintong Aklat Award (2006) and was shortlisted for the ALS Gold Medal (snippet) — [Wikipedia (snippet)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merlinda_Bobis)
- Publishers Weekly praised its "compassion and tenderness"; US edition ISBN 9780385341127 (Delacorte, 2008) — [Publishers Weekly](https://www2.publishersweekly.com/9780385341127)
- Reviewed in [Philstar, 2005, "Heart of the matter"](https://philstar.com/arts-and-culture/2005/12/05/310708/heart-matter). Scholarship: [Kunapipi 2010](https://ro.uow.edu.au/kunapipi/vol32/iss1/16), [UPLB journal](https://www.ukdr.uplb.edu.ph/journal-articles/5314)
- Suggested summary (original): The summer she turns twelve, the eldest daughter of a poor family on a Bicol street goes looking for "heart food", the love she can't get at home, in her neighbors' kitchens.
- Conflict: the narrator is "Nenita" in most listings and "Nining" in the Diliman Review.

**3. *Mga Kuwento ni Lola Basyang*, Severino Reyes (stories from 1925; book editions 1997, 2005). Children's stories, Filipino (English translation exists). No setIn**
- Reyes started the series in 1925 for the Tagalog magazine *Liwayway* under the pen name "Lola Basyang". It ran from 1925 to 1942 — [Wikipedia (snippet)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mga_Kuwento_ni_Lola_Basyang); [Plaridel journal, Bellen](https://www.plarideljournal.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/10/feb_2009_v6n1_03bellen_magkukuwento_na_si_lola.pdf)
- Tahanan Books edition: 12 stories selected and edited by Christine S. Bellen, illustrated by Felix Mago Miguel, ©2005, 222 pp. — [HNU library record](https://lrc.hnu.edu.ph/bib/135597). English edition: translated by Gilda Cordero-Fernando, illustrated by Albert Gamos, Tahanan 1997, reprinted 2025 — [Yuchengco Museum shop](https://yuchengcomuseum.org/product/the-best-of-lola-basyang-timeless-tales-for-the-filipino-family/)
- Still current: adapted for TV, and covered by [Manila Bulletin, 2025](https://mb.com.ph/2025/05/24/honoring-the-legacy-of-lola-basyang-and-todays-storytellers)
- Suggested summary (original): A grandmother tells bedtime tales of princes, enchanted kingdoms and clever children. These are the stories generations of Filipino kids grew up hearing.

**4. *Margosatubig: The Story of Salagunting*, Ramon L. Muzones (serialized 1946; English translation 2012). Novel, Hiligaynon (English by Ma. Cecilia Locsin-Nava). Possible setIn: none confirmed**
- Muzones was named National Artist for Literature posthumously in 2018 and wrote about 61–62 novels — [NCCA](https://ncca.gov.ph/?p=18805); [Central Philippine University](https://cpu.edu.ph/news/malacanang-hails-centralian-as-national-artist-for-literature/)
- *Margosatubig* is his best-known work and is "considered the first Hiligaynon bestseller". It ran in *Yuhum* magazine in 1946, in 30 installments — [Wikipedia (snippet)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramon_Muzones); [Balay Sugidanun](https://balaysugidanun.com/2012/06/29/hiligaynon-novel-margosatubig-by-ramon-muzones-now-in-english/)
- English edition published by Ateneo de Manila University Press in 2012, with a foreword by National Artist Virgilio S. Almario — [Ateneo Press listing](https://unipress.ateneo.edu/node/455); [UP library record](https://tuklas.up.edu.ph/Record/UP-99796217612423616)
- Plot: a talismanned young hero, Salagunting, fights to win back his father's kingdom. Scholars read it as an allegory of the fight for independence — [UP journal article (snippet)](https://journals.upd.edu.ph/index.php/jescl/article/download/6220/5510)
- Suggested summary (original): A prince raised far from his throne grows up with magic on his side and sets out to win back the kingdom his father lost. On the way he finds romance, rivals and a deadly spear.
- Setting: sources say it is a West Visayan, precolonial fantasy. Despite the title (also a Zamboanga del Sur town name), no source tied it to Mindanao, so don't link it to `zamboanga-city-mindanao` or `iloilo-guimaras` without confirming.

**5. *Isabela*, Kaisa Aquino (2024). Novel, English. No guide exists for Isabela province**
- Won Best Novel in English at the 43rd National Book Awards (NBDB and Filipino Critics Circle). Winners were announced in February 2026 and honored on March 14, 2026 — [Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2026/03/13/here-are-the-winners-of-the-43rd-national-book-awards); [GMA](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/lifestyle/artandculture/977134/43rd-national-book-awards-list-of-winners/story)
- Debut novel from Ateneo de Manila University Press. It weaves the voices of women (mother, daughter, wife, lover, teacher, cadre, revolutionary) across about three decades of Isabela's history — [Ateneo feature](https://cms-training.ateneo.edu/news/2024/09/24/fragmented-realities-lost-histories-exploring-women-revolution-grief-kaisa-aquinos); [Ateneo Archium](https://archium.ateneo.edu/fine-arts-faculty-pubs/20)
- Its first chapters were written at the 2019 Silliman (Dumaguete) Writers Workshop — [Ateneo "Hot Press"](https://cms-training.ateneo.edu/features/2024/05/20/hot-press-isabela)
- Reviewed by [Inquirer Lifestyle](https://lifestyle.inquirer.net/?p=507047) (could not fetch the text)
- Suggested summary (original): Women across three decades in the northern Luzon province of Isabela tell overlapping stories of love, grief and revolution.

**6. *Hudhud hi Aliguyon*, Ifugao oral epic, translated by Amador T. Daguio (1952 thesis; later editions incl. 1976). Epic poetry, Ifugao/English. setIn: `banaue-rice-terraces-ifugao`**
- UNESCO proclaimed the Hudhud chants of the Ifugao a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity in 2001 and inscribed them on the Representative List in 2008. The chants number 200+ with 40 episodes each and are tied to rice sowing, harvest and funerals, and they are mostly chanted by elderly women — [UNESCO ICH](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/hudhud-chants-of-the-ifugao-00015); [Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/good-news/644254/hudhud-ifugao-enchanting-chanting)
- Daguio's 1952 Stanford M.A. thesis gives the text and verse translation of an Ifugao harvest song, with notes. The text was gathered around 1939 by an Ifugao schoolteacher — [Yale eHRAF](https://ehrafworldcultures.yale.edu/cultures/oa19/documents/010); [UP library](https://tuklas.up.edu.ph/Record/UP-99796217611916645?lng=fr)
- Suggested summary (original): Aliguyon, son of a village leader, goes to war with a rival from a neighboring village. Their fight lasts years. A rice-harvest chant still sung in the terraces.
- Note: the plot outline (a long duel ending in friendship) is general knowledge, not confirmed by a fetched source. Keep the summary at the setup.

**7. *Biag ni Lam-ang*, Ilocano oral epic (first printed 1889–90). Epic poetry, Ilocano. Possible setIn: `vigan-and-laoag-ilocos` (regional link only)**
- Described as the first Philippine folk epic recorded in writing. Fr. Gerardo Blanco's text was published by Isabelo de los Reyes in *El Ilocano*, December 1889 to February 1890 — [Wikipedia (snippet)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biag_ni_Lam-ang); [UP Library rare periodicals](https://repository.mainlib.upd.edu.ph/omekas/s/rare-periodicals/media/150311)
- Attribution to the blind poet Pedro Bukaneg (1592–c.1630) is traditional and uncertain. A 1961 claim that he wrote it down in 1640 conflicts with his death date — [Wikipedia, Pedro Bucaneg (snippet)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Bucaneg)
- Suggested summary (original): A boy who can talk from birth sets off to find his missing father, and then to win the woman he loves. The story mixes heroics with Ilocano humor.
- Needs a specific modern edition (publisher, translator) before adding. None was found here.

**8. *Ako ang Daigdig*, Alejandro G. Abadilla (title poem 1940). Poetry, Tagalog. No setIn**
- The free-verse poem of 1940 broke with metered, rhymed Tagalog verse. Abadilla is called the "Father of Modern Tagalog Poetry" (critic Pedro Ricarte) — [Bienvenido Lumbera via JOLL](https://e-journal.usd.ac.id/index.php/JOLL/article/download/390/337); [Tagalog Lang](https://www.tagaloglang.com/alejandro-g-abadilla)
- Member of Kapisanang Panitikan (1935), which held a symbolic book burning at Plaza Moriones, Tondo, on March 2, 1940 — [Wikipedia (snippet)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_G._Abadilla)
- Conflict: Wikipedia dates the poem to 1955. Lumbera and the anthology date it to 1940. The book collection's year and publisher were not confirmed.

**9. *His Native Coast*, Edith L. Tiempo (1979). Novel, English. setIn unconfirmed**
- Published by New Day (Quezon City, 1979, about 300 pp.), with a UP Press edition in 2000 — [Ortigas Foundation Library](https://www.ortigasfoundationlibrary.com.ph/collections/library-holdings/his-native-coast-novel); [Cambridge / Journal of Asian Studies review listing](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-asian-studies/article/threecornered-sun-by-tycasper-linda-quezon-city-new-day-publishers-1979-232-pp-glossary-825-paper-distributed-by-the-cellar-bookshop-essays-on-the-philippine-novel-in-english-edited-by-galdon-joseph-a-quezon-city-ateneo-de-manila-university-press-1979-vii-168-pp-index-1750-cloth-850-paper-distributed-by-the-cellar-bookshop-his-native-coast-by-tiempo-edith-quezon-city-new-day-publishers-1979-iv-298-pp-450-paper-distributed-by-the-cellar-bookshop/B899BA44785D23824EC9B34DC2F9CB78)
- Studied in [Philippine Studies (Genotiva)](https://archium.ateneo.edu/phstudies/vol45/iss2/5) and [Review of Women's Studies (Ventura)](https://www.journals.upd.edu.ph/index.php/rws/article/view/2867)
- Tiempo co-founded the Silliman National Writers Workshop in Dumaguete and died there in 2011 — [Ateneo ALIWW](https://rizaldb.ateneo.edu/aliww/english_etiempo.html); [Silliman University](https://su.edu.ph/237-dwelling-in-possibilities/)
- Do **not** set `setIn: dumaguete-apo-island` yet: no source confirmed the novel is set there.

**10. *Ang Mga Tunoc sang Isa ca Bulac* (The Thorns of a Flower), Magdalena Jalandoni (written 1907). Novel, Hiligaynon. Weak candidate**
- Jalandoni (1891–1978), born in Jaro, Iloilo City, was a novelist and feminist. Her first novel dates to 1907 — [Encyclopedia.com](https://encyclopedia.com/women/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/jalandoni-magdalena-1891-1978); [Iloilo eLibrary](https://iloiloelibrary.com/items/show/838)
- Title spellings vary (Tunoc/Tunok/Tunuk, Bulac/Bulak/Bulaklak). No in-print edition, publisher or plot summary was found, so this one is not ready to add.

### Inferences
- Best fits for the shelf now: *Portrait* (Intramuros guide), *Banana Heart Summer* (Bicol, once the Albay link is confirmed), *Lola Basyang* (the shelf has no classic children's book in Filipino), *Margosatubig* (first Hiligaynon book on the shelf, by a National Artist), and *Isabela* (2026 prizewinner).
- The Hudhud and *Biag ni Lam-ang* are oral epics, so each needs a specific edition (translator, publisher) to pass the book page's `author`/`year` fields. Credit them as "Ifugao oral tradition, trans. Amador T. Daguio" and similar.
- Gap still open: no Cebuano-language book found with a findable, in-print edition. Marcel Navarra's *Mga Piling Kuwentong Sebuwano* (UP Press, 1986) is the nearest candidate — [UP library](https://tuklas.up.edu.ph/Record/UP-1685594773861589528/Description); [Cebuano Studies Center](https://www.cebuanostudiescenter.com/?p=317) (Navarra is called the "Father of Modern Cebuano Literature").

## 3. National Book Awards Philippines 2026 and international prizes

### Takeaway
