# Traveler Search Intents and Information Needs Across the Trip Lifecycle

Research date: 2026-11-17. Data is 2023-2026 unless noted. Several key pages (Think with Google, Taylor & Francis, TravelPulse, advertising.expedia.com) blocked fetching or redirected, so some figures come from search snippets or syndicated copies. Those are flagged.

## 1. Most common destination search intents and how they rank

### Takeaway
No public source ranks destination query types ("best time to visit", "things to do", "is X safe" and so on) by global search volume. The best ranking comes from destination-choice surveys. Weather/seasonality, cost, trip type/activities, how to get there and travel time lead among travelers comparing destinations. Safety ranks first in several US surveys. Price dominates the booking stage. SEO practitioners treat "things to do in X" and "best time to visit X" as the core informational destination keywords, but they publish no comparative volumes.

### Cited Findings
**Destination-choice factors (the best proxy for intent ranking)**
- Expedia Group (Path to Purchase study with Luth Research, 2023, plus Q1 2024 Expedia insight data), among travelers considering multiple destinations: weather or seasonality 53%, trip type (beach, activity, city, cruise) 48%, travel costs 47%, how to get there 46%, travel time to arrive 45% — [Hospitality Net / Expedia Group Media Solutions, Jul 2024](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4123087.html)
- 59% of travelers were undecided on a destination, or were weighing several, when they first decided to travel (over 80% in Mexico, 62% in the UK). Only 20% considered destinations in similar regions — [Hospitality Net / Expedia](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4123087.html); [Expedia Group partner blog, Jan 2024](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/resources/blog/path-to-purchase-insights)
- LocaliQ 2026 travel study (1,900+ US adults who traveled in the past 12 months): 91% rate safety extremely or very important when choosing a destination. 64% look for destinations within driving range, 63% want family-friendly places, 57% couples-friendly, and 55% somewhere near national parks — [LocaliQ, updated Jun 2026](https://localiq.com/blog/travel-marketing-2026/)
- LocaliQ: of travelers seeking safety information, 72% look for crime and security updates and 61% for health concerns. The most influential content types are deals and offers (67%), destination guides (51%), travel tips and safety info (46%), and personal travel stories (45%) — [LocaliQ](https://localiq.com/blog/travel-marketing-2026/)
- Global Rescue (Jan 2025): 50% of experienced travelers rank safety as the most important factor in choosing an international destination — [Global Rescue](https://www.globalrescue.com/common/blog/detail/risk-averse-tech-savvy-travelers-trend/) (search snippet; sample is experienced/risk-aware travelers, which likely skews toward safety)
- Safer Tourism Foundation poll (2,000 people): cost was the top priority (75%), then destination type (65%) and weather (63%) — [weather2travel](https://weather2travel.com/news/holiday-head-research-shows-cost-and-weather-priorities) (search snippet; survey date unclear)
- Talker Research: what mattered most for 2024 trips was the destination itself (53%), cost (48%) and experiences/activities (30%). For 2025, cost leads (52%) and destination is second (49%) — [Talker Research](https://talkerresearch.com/these-are-the-travel-trends-that-shaped-2024/) (search snippet)
- Booking Holdings Gen Z survey (Jan-Feb 2024): delicious local food, good value for money, and natural scenery ranked top, with safety after them — reported in search results for [TravelPulse / related coverage](https://www.travelpulse.com/news/features/key-factors-influencing-american-travelers-destination-decisions) (snippet only, not verified on the primary page)
- YouGov (with Tripadvisor involvement): 51% of US adults call the destination/location the most crucial planning factor, experiences/activities come second (17%), and accommodation third (12%) — [YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/52642-reality-checks-talkshow-hilary-fischer-groban-travel-experiences?market=contact) (snippet)
- Tripadvisor 2024 Experience of Travel study (7 markets): 67% value experiences more than things — [Skift, Nov 2023](https://skift.com/2023/11/16/tripadvisors-2024-experience-of-travel-study-3-charts)

**Booking-stage priorities**
- What drives the booking decision: best price 49%, a positive previous experience with the brand 31%, flexible cancellation or refundable rates 28% — [Expedia Group partner blog, Jan 2024](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/resources/blog/path-to-purchase-insights)
- 72% always or frequently read reviews before choosing places to stay, eat or things to do. 81% of Tripadvisor users say reviews are important when booking attractions. Recency of reviews matters most — [Lodging Magazine](https://lodgingmagazine.com/study-reveals-impact-of-online-reviews-when-booking-trips/) (older Tripadvisor/Phocuswright data, likely pre-2022)

**Keyword patterns from search and SEO sources**
- Google: searches for "travel itinerary" (and "solo travel") hit an all-time high in January 2024 (US, mid-Jan to mid-Mar). "Things to do" + "with kids" was a leading query pattern, with Orlando ranked first — secondary coverage of Google data via [Yahoo / Google Flights coverage](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/13-summer-trips-americans-searching-143825396.html) and [Think with Google summer 2024](https://thinkwithgoogle.com/intl/en-emea/consumer-insights/consumer-trends/summer-2024-travel-trends) (the primary page was not fetched)
- Kantar, UK Google Trends: "things to do in Paris" had the highest year-over-year interest growth (+6%) in its set — [Kantar](https://www.kantar.com/uki/inspiration/hospitality-travel/summer-travel-insights-the-story-behind-the-search-data)
- Ahrefs suggests building destination content hubs with "why visit" and "when is the best time to visit" subpages. Its examples use "things to do in seoul", "seoul food" and "[country] travel guide" as core destination keywords — [Ahrefs blog-niche](https://ahrefs.com/blog/blog-niche); [Ahrefs travel SEO](https://ahrefs.com/blog/travel-seo)
- Informational queries such as "best time to visit Japan" and "things to do in Paris" are a separate intent class from transactional ones such as "book Maldives resort" — [CausalFunnel](https://www.causalfunnel.com/blog/travel-seo-keywords-the-complete-data-driven-guide-to-ranking-in-2026/)
- Travelpayouts' list of high-search UK/US long-tail travel keywords (updated Nov 2023, no volumes or method given) is dominated by these patterns: flights to [destination] from [origin], "is it safe to travel to [country]", passport/visa/entry questions, travel insurance, audience-fit queries ("best beaches for families/over 50s"), and price modifiers ("cheap", "all inclusive", "last minute") — [Travelpayouts](https://www.travelpayouts.com/blog/long-tail-travel-keywords-in-the-uk-and-usa/)

### Inferences
- A rough importance ranking for a destination guide, combining the surveys above: (1) why go / what kind of trip it is (trip type, experiences, scenery); (2) best time to visit / weather; (3) cost and budget; (4) how to get there and travel time; (5) safety; (6) things to do; (7) where to stay; (8) food; (9) getting around; (10) practical items (entry/visa, money, SIM, etiquette, packing). Things to do probably carries the highest raw search volume for a named destination (it is the canonical SEO head term). The surveys place it below weather and cost as a deciding factor, so it should come early but not first.
- 59% of travelers start without a destination and compare about three. A guide therefore has to sell the destination before it details it: lead with a "why go / who it's for" hook and the best-time and cost facts that comparison shoppers screen on. Only 20% compare similar regions, so cross-destination comparisons ("X vs Y") are worth adding.
- For island or regional destinations like the Philippines, "how to get there" and "travel time" (46% and 45%) are probably weighted more heavily than average, because of inter-island flights and ferries. That is an inference, not measured data.

### Gaps
- No public dataset ranks the query types ("best time to visit X" vs "things to do in X" vs "is X safe") by volume for one destination. Getting that would mean pulling Ahrefs or Semrush keyword data for specific destinations (for example "Palawan", "Siargao").
- I could not get the Statista global "important factors when choosing travel destinations" chart (Mar 2024) in full. Snippets say safety is top, but the percentages were not visible.
- The original Think with Google travel statistics page now redirects to business.google.com/think, and its figures could not be retrieved.

## 2. How information needs differ between planning and in-destination

### Takeaway
Pre-trip research is long and multi-source: about 45 days, 141 pages and about 5 hours (8.7 hours in the US), using OTAs, search engines, social media and destination sites. In-destination needs move to mobile, location-based, immediate decisions: maps and wayfinding, "near me" food and things to do, opening hours, reviews. Many travelers leave part of the itinerary to decide on the spot.

### Cited Findings
**Planning phase**
- In the 45 days before booking, travelers view 141 travel content pages on average and spend 303 minutes (524 minutes in the US). Sample: 70,000+ tracked travelers and 5,700+ survey respondents in 7 markets (AU, CA, FR, JP, MX, UK, US) — [Expedia Group partner blog, Jan 2024](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/resources/blog/path-to-purchase-insights)
- Sources used before purchase: OTAs 80%, search engines 61%, social media 58%, airline sites 54% — [Expedia Group](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/resources/blog/path-to-purchase-insights)
- Resources by phase (among users of each). Inspiration: destination websites 45%, meta sites 36%, OTAs 35%, and 77% of social media users use it for inspiration. Research: search engines 72%, meta sites 69%, social 69%, destination websites 68%. Planning: OTAs 44%, meta 38% — [Expedia Group](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/resources/blog/path-to-purchase-insights)
- 68% used destination sites for research (80% in Japan), and over 40% of those said the sites influenced their decision. In the planning phase, destination-site use drops to 27%. Social media was used for destination inspiration by 65%; 40% viewed pictures or videos and 35% sought feedback from friends. Social platforms used in planning: Instagram 65%, Facebook 48%, YouTube 40%, TikTok 37% — [Hospitality Net / Expedia, Jul 2024](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4123087.html)
- The 180+ day search window grew 20% (Q2 2024 Expedia insights) — [Hospitality Net / Expedia](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4123087.html)
- Go City survey: travelers spend 17h42m planning each trip, including 3h54m researching and booking experiences. Another report found 36% spend more than five hours searching and comparing before booking — search-result summary of [TravelPulse](https://www.travelpulse.com/news/features/travel-planning-has-become-overwhelming-report-shows) (page returned 403; figures not verified on the primary page)
- Skift: travelers pass through about 65 touchpoints before booking, up from 45 in 2018 — [Skift report](https://skift.com/?p=838634) (snippet)
- LocaliQ 2026 planning sources: travel sites (Tripadvisor, Expedia) 61%, up from 56% in 2024; friends and family 51%; social media 43%. Trust: friends and family 95%, travel sites 92%, social 78% — [LocaliQ](https://localiq.com/blog/travel-marketing-2026/)
- Aven Hospitality / Talker Research (Oct 2026, 4,000 US/UK/AU travelers): 67% have used AI tools in travel searches, 68% to compare trip options, and 99% plan to use AI for planning — [Hotel-Online](https://www.hotel-online.com/news/travel-mishaps-cost-travelers-nearly-2500)

**In-destination phase**
- Kang, Jodice & Norman (2019, *Tourism Recreation Research*): the categories of tourism information searched on smartphones differed significantly between before the trip and during it — [Taylor & Francis abstract](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/02508281.2019.1627076) (pre-2022; full category list not retrieved, page returned 403)
- Visitor survey (Aberdeen destination body): on holiday, the second most common smartphone use is opening maps for wayfinding and places to visit (56%), and 46% use it to find general tourist information. It advises businesses to keep opening times, location and booking info clear and current — [VisitAberdeenshire industry](https://industry.visitabdn.com/news/visitor-insights-highlight-why-mobile-friendly-business-information-matters) (search snippet; date and sample not verified)
- Tripadvisor Summer Travel Index (May 2024, 2,400+ consumers): 56% of US travelers decide at least part of their itinerary "in the moment" — [Tripadvisor mediaroom](https://tripadvisor.mediaroom.com/2024-05-14-TRIPADVISOR-PREDICTS-BUSY-SUMMER-TRAVEL-SEASON-FOR-AMERICANS-AN-INCREASING-DESIRE-FOR-ADVENTURE)
- PolyU (Nov 2024) process-tracking study of group holidays (97 decision scenarios): smartphones drove on-trip choices about activities, meals and shopping. Group members searched for restaurants separately and picked places with good reviews across result sets — [PolyU SHTM](https://www.polyu.edu.hk/shtm/news-and-events/news/2024/20241127_shape-travel-decisions/)
- Lund University thesis (experience sampling): four themes in in-trip smartphone search were flexible plans, orientation in time and space, specific queries, and aiming for optimization — [Lund University](https://lup.lub.lu.se/record/d2dba839-2475-4c4d-b8c3-98be24e03879)
- Google (older, about 2016-2017): mobile searches for "things to do" + "near me" grew 6x over two years ("things to do near me today/this weekend"). Smartphone searches from hotels rose about 30% year over year. "Places to eat near me" more than tripled in a year (Australia) — [Think with Google, summer marketing](https://thinkwithgoogle.com/consumer-insights/summer-marketing); [Think with Google explore moments](https://thinkwithgoogle.com/consumer-insights/mobile-influence-travel-decision-making-explore-moments) (pre-2022 data, flagged as old)
- Most common on-trip mishaps: bad weather 26%, illness or injury 16%, phone dying or losing internet 14%, getting lost 14% — [Hotel-Online / Aven-Talker, Oct 2026](https://www.hotel-online.com/news/travel-mishaps-cost-travelers-nearly-2500)

### Inferences
- A guide does two jobs. It is a long-form planning read (desktop, deep, compared against other sources) and an on-trip reference (mobile, skimmable). On-trip use favors a jump-to table of contents, maps or map links, getting-around details, food by area, and practical info someone can check in seconds (opening hours, fares, ferry schedules, cash/ATM availability).
- "Phone dying / losing internet" (14%) and "getting lost" (14%) are common mishaps, which supports including connectivity/SIM/eSIM advice and offline-map tips. That matters especially for islands with patchy coverage.
- Bad weather is the top mishap (26%) and weather/seasonality is the top destination-choice factor (53%). Weather therefore matters both before the trip (when to go) and during it (typhoon/rainy-season contingencies, ferry cancellations).
- 56% leave part of the itinerary to decide "in the moment", which favors "options by area" and "rainy-day alternatives" over rigid hour-by-hour itineraries alone.

### Gaps
- No current (2022+) Google data on "near me" or "open now" travel query growth was found. The Think with Google figures are from about 2016-2017.
- The full category breakdown from Kang et al. (2019) could not be retrieved.

## 3. Top frustrations and missing information in travel planning

### Takeaway
The main frustrations are information overload and fragmentation (too many options and sources, about 65 touchpoints), hard-to-find or confusing information, and inaccurate or outdated details that cause on-trip problems. Cost is the biggest barrier to traveling at all.

### Cited Findings
- Accenture (May 2024): travelers feel overwhelmed by the volume of content and advertising from competing brands and influencers, and planning feels "siloed, disjointed and overwhelming" — search-result summary of [TravelPulse](https://www.travelpulse.com/news/features/travel-planning-has-become-overwhelming-report-shows) and [Travelweek](https://www.travelweek.ca/?p=170100) (primary pages not fetched)
- A survey of 1,200+ people found 50% had run into confusing sites where information was hard to find — search-result summary ([Travel News SA](https://www.travelnews.co.za/article/direct-booking-sites-stress-travellers-survey)) (snippet)
- Aven Hospitality / Talker Research (Oct 2026, n=4,000): 73% have had a major travel mishap. 51% blame a lack of accurate planning or booking information. Inaccurate or outdated information cost an average of $1,891. 14% found an advertised amenity unavailable, 13% booked falsely advertised accommodation, and 85% permanently changed how they plan afterward — [Hotel-Online](https://www.hotel-online.com/news/travel-mishaps-cost-travelers-nearly-2500)
- Amadeus "Friction Removed" (Nov 2024, US): delays and cancellations are the top distress (68%), then combined travel costs (50%). Route and schedule-finding distressed 49% of Gen Z vs 35% of those 65+ — [10 Minutes News for Hoteliers](https://en.10minhotel.com/2024/11/20/amadeus-delays-cancellations-and-costs-cause-friction-for-u-s-travelers)
- Travelport/Dynata (Aug 2026): almost half of American travelers have abandoned bookings because the process was too complicated — [Destination.com](https://www.destination.com/news/us-travelers-abandon-bookings-complexity-travelport-survey) (snippet)
- LocaliQ 2026: cost is the top obstacle to traveling more (68%, up from 65%), followed by safety concerns (36%) and the hassle of air travel (29%) — [LocaliQ](https://localiq.com/blog/travel-marketing-2026/)
- Reviews: recency is the most important thing readers look for (about 78% focus on recent reviews) — [Lodging Magazine](https://lodgingmagazine.com/study-reveals-impact-of-online-reviews-when-booking-trips/) (older data)

### Inferences
- Guide design implications: (a) consolidate the scattered essentials on one page so the reader needs fewer touchpoints; (b) show a visible "last updated" date and current prices, since recency and accuracy are top concerns; (c) give concrete budget numbers, because cost is the number-one barrier and among the top two choice factors; (d) explain multi-leg routes clearly (how to get there, transfer times), because route and schedule-finding is a named pain point that is probably worse for island-hopping.
- Honest location and accommodation details (distance to the main areas, what is actually nearby) respond directly to the "falsely advertised / inaccurate location" complaints.

### Gaps
- No survey found that asks specifically what travelers find missing in *blog or destination-guide* content, as opposed to booking sites. The frustration data above is mostly about planning or booking in general.
- The Go City, Accenture and "1,200+ people" figures came through search summaries and could not be verified on primary pages (403 errors).

## 4. Recurring question patterns (People Also Ask / autocomplete)

### Takeaway
No published dataset quantifies People Also Ask or autocomplete patterns for destinations. Practitioner sources and observed SERP templates consistently show these patterns: "is X safe", "best time to visit X", "things to do in X", "how many days in X", "is X worth it/visiting", "X vs Y", "how to get to X", "X itinerary", "is X expensive / how much does a trip to X cost", plus audience-fit variants ("X for families", "is X safe for solo female travelers").

### Cited Findings
- "Is [destination] safe?" is described as one of the most common questions in travel communities, and Google results for it are formulaic — [Medium, Wanderstruck Ella](https://medium.com/@wanderstruckella/stop-asking-is-it-safe-to-travel-to-67ebe5bfb144); [One Mile at a Time](https://onemileatatime.com/insights/travel-destination-safe/)
- Programmatic safety sites build per-city page clusters around variants such as "Is X safe to travel alone?", "Is X safe for families?" and "Is X safe right now?", which suggests they are targeting recurring PAA variants — [The World Travel Index (example)](https://theworldtravelindex.com/en/europe/russia-europe/astrakhan/is-astrakhan-safe)
- "Is it safe to travel to [country]" appears among frequently searched long-tail travel keywords, along with passport-validity and entry questions — [Travelpayouts, Nov 2023](https://www.travelpayouts.com/blog/long-tail-travel-keywords-in-the-uk-and-usa/)
- Recommended keyword-discovery methods for travel (Google Suggest/autocomplete, Related Searches, "People also search for", Quora) — [Travelpayouts](https://www.travelpayouts.com/blog/long-tail-travel-keywords-in-the-uk-and-usa/)
- "Best time to visit *" is used as a seed pattern for informational guides — [LowFruits](https://lowfruits.io/4mma) (snippet); "X Things to do in [place]" as a standard content format — [Ranktracker](https://ranktracker.com/blog/different-types-of-travel-keywords-and-modifiers-explained)
- Trip length ("How much time do you have?") is framed as a first planning decision in general planning guides — search-result summary (TourRadar), not a quantitative source
- "Travel itinerary" searches hit an all-time high in Jan 2024 — [Think with Google summer 2024 coverage](https://thinkwithgoogle.com/intl/en-emea/consumer-insights/consumer-trends/summer-2024-travel-trends) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Each recurring question is a natural FAQ entry and H2/H3 candidate. A Philippines island guide FAQ should probably cover: Is [island] worth visiting? How many days do you need? Is it safe? When is the best time / typhoon season? How do I get there from Manila/Cebu? How much does it cost per day? [Island] vs [nearby island]? Do I need cash / are there ATMs? Is there good mobile signal/Wi-Fi? Short, direct first-sentence answers fit PAA-style snippet extraction and the site's existing FAQPage JSON-LD.
- "X vs Y" is under-documented in the data, but Expedia's finding that travelers compare about three destinations supports adding comparison sections or links (for example El Nido vs Coron, Siargao vs Siquijor).

### Gaps
- No quantitative PAA or autocomplete frequency data for destination queries was found. Getting it would mean running a SERP/PAA scraper or Ahrefs/Semrush "Questions" reports on target destinations. The patterns above rely on practitioner observation and SERP templates, not measured frequency.
- No data was found on how often "is X worth it" or "how many days in X" appear compared with "is X safe".
