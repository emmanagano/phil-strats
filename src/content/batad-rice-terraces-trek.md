If you visit the Banaue Rice Terraces, make time for Batad. An hour from Banaue town, this village sits at the bottom of a natural amphitheater of stone-walled terraces, one of the five clusters that make up the UNESCO-listed Rice Terraces of the Philippine Cordilleras. There's no road into the village, so you arrive on foot, which is half the magic. This guide covers the trek, costs and where to stay. For transport from Manila and the rest of the area, see our [Banaue Rice Terraces guide](/articles/banaue-rice-terraces-ifugao).

## Why Batad is worth the effort

The Banaue viewpoint shows you terraces from a roadside platform. Batad puts you inside them. The terraces rise in a near-perfect bowl around the village, still farmed by Ifugao families with techniques passed down for generations. You'll walk along terrace walls, past rice paddies and native houses, with the sound of water running through bamboo channels.

## Getting from Banaue to Batad

| Step | How | Time | Cost (≈) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Banaue town → Batad saddle | Tricycle, jeepney to the junction, or hired van | 45 min–1 hr | ₱50–150 pp by jeepney; ₱800–1,500 per tricycle round trip |
| Saddle → Batad village | Walk down a paved path and steps | 30–60 min | Free |
| Village → terraces and falls | Walk with a guide | 2–4 hrs return | ₱500–1,000 per guide |

Jeepney times are informal, so ask your Banaue inn the night before. A hired tricycle can wait at the saddle while you trek.

## The Batad trek, step by step

1. **Pay the fees.** Expect a small Banaue tourism fee (≈ ₱20) and the Batad environmental fee (≈ ₱50–100) on arrival.
2. **Walk down from the saddle.** The path drops through forest to the viewpoint above the village, where the whole amphitheater opens up.
3. **Cross the terraces.** With a guide, follow the terrace walls down through the village and across the bowl. Walls can be narrow and slippery, so take it slowly.
4. **Continue to Tappiya Falls.** About an hour beyond the village, a steep stairway leads down to a tall waterfall with a cold swimming pool.
5. **Climb back up.** This is the hard part: hundreds of stone steps back to the village and then to the saddle. Leave plenty of time before dark.

## Tappiya Falls

Tappiya Falls drops into a deep, cold pool beneath the cliffs. It's a refreshing swim on a hot afternoon, but the water is chilly and the current near the falls is strong. Most people treat it as a half-day add-on from the village.

## Guides and fees

- **Guide:** ≈ ₱500–1,000 for the terraces and Tappiya Falls, booked in Banaue or Batad
- **Environmental fee:** ≈ ₱50–100 per person in Batad
- **Banaue tourism fee:** ≈ ₱20
- **Porters:** available for luggage if you're staying overnight; agree on the price first

Bring cash in small bills: there are no ATMs in Batad.

## Where to stay in Batad

Simple homestays and family-run inns line the terraces, many with balconies over the amphitheater. Rooms are basic, often with shared bathrooms and cold showers, from about ₱500–1,500 a night. Book ahead in peak months, and bring a light jacket: nights are cool.

## Best time to visit Batad

March to May has the driest trails. The terraces are greenest from around June, but rain makes the stone steps slick, and July to September brings typhoon season and landslide risk on the road from Banaue. The planting and harvest cycle varies by village, so ask locally if you want to see the fields flooded or golden.

## Respect the terraces

The terraces are farms and homes, not a theme park. Stay on the walls and paths, never walk through planted paddies, ask before photographing people, and carry out all your trash. Buying meals and crafts in the village puts money directly into the community that maintains the terraces.
