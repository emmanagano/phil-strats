Sabtang is where Batanes' Ivatan heritage feels most alive. A short boat ride from Batan Island, this small island has stone villages with thick limestone walls and cogon roofs, white-sand coves and a famous natural arch at Morong Beach. Most travelers see it on a day trip, and it's often the highlight of a Batanes trip. This guide covers the boat, fees and the best route around the island. For flights, budgets and the rest of the province, see our [Batanes travel guide](/articles/batanes-islands).

## How to get to Sabtang

1. **Get to Ivana Port early.** It's about 30 minutes south of Basco by tricycle or van. Be there by about 6–6:30am.
2. **Take the faluwa.** These sturdy, open boats cross the channel in about 30–45 minutes for roughly ₱100 each way.
3. **Register in Sabtang.** Pay the environmental fee at the tourism office near the port.
4. **Hire a tricycle.** Drivers wait at the port for island tours.

Boats run on weather, not timetables. If the sea is rough, crossings are cancelled, so plan Sabtang for your first calm morning and keep another day free.

## Sabtang tricycle tour route

| Stop | What to see |
|---|---|
| **Sabtang town & San Vicente Ferrer Church** | A white-washed stone church and the island's small main village |
| **Savidug** | Stone houses lining a quiet lane, still lived in |
| **Chavayan** | The best-preserved Ivatan village, with stone houses, a little chapel, and vakul (head covers) woven from palm fiber |
| **Morong Beach & Nakabuang Arch** | White sand, turquoise water and a natural stone arch: the classic Sabtang photo |
| **Viewpoints along the coast road** | Cliffs, green hills and the open sea |

A tricycle tour takes four to five hours and costs roughly ₱800–1,500 per tricycle, depending on the season and your route. Agree on the stops and the price before you set off.

## Chavayan: the stone village

Chavayan sits at the end of the road on Sabtang's south side, between steep hills and the sea. Its houses are built from coral and limestone, with walls up to a meter thick and roofs of thatched cogon grass: a design that has survived Batanes' typhoons for generations. Locals sell vakul, the woven head covers Ivatan farmers wear against sun and rain, and you can try one on for photos. Remember that this is a working village: ask before photographing people or homes.

## Morong Beach and the Nakabuang Arch

Morong Beach is a long stretch of white sand with clear, shallow water, framed by the Nakabuang Arch, a natural rock formation. It's the best place in Batanes for a swim on a calm day. Small stalls sell lunch, including fresh seafood and turmeric rice. Currents can be strong, so swim close to shore.

## Fees and costs

- **Faluwa boat:** ≈ ₱100 each way
- **Sabtang environmental fee:** ≈ ₱200–350 per person
- **Tricycle tour:** ≈ ₱800–1,500 per tricycle
- **Lunch:** ≈ ₱200–400 per person
- **Batan transfer to Ivana Port:** ≈ ₱300–600 per tricycle

There are no ATMs on Sabtang, so bring enough cash for the whole day.

## Tips for Sabtang

- Take motion-sickness tablets before the boat if you're prone to it. The channel can be choppy.
- Pack a dry bag. Spray from the boat soaks bags and phones.
- Bring sunscreen and a hat. There's little shade on the coast road.
- Stay overnight in a homestay if you want the villages to yourself after the day-trippers leave.
- Leave no trash. Batanes keeps its villages and beaches remarkably clean.

## Best time to visit Sabtang

March to May is the most reliable window, with calm seas and clear skies. From June to October, typhoons and swells can cancel the boat for days. The cooler months from November to February bring strong northeast winds, which also make crossings rough. Whenever you go, leave buffer days in your Batanes plans.
