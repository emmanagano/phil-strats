Sagada's limestone hills hide some of the most famous caves in the Philippines. Sumaguing, the "Big Cave", is a barefoot scramble past rimstone pools and giant formations. Lumiang is a burial cave, its mouth stacked with old coffins. The **cave connection** joins the two underground. This guide helps you pick the right one, book a guide and stay safe. For the rest of your trip, see our full [Sagada travel guide](/articles/sagada-mountain-province).

## Sagada caves at a glance

| Cave | What it's like | Time | Difficulty | Guide fee (≈) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sumaguing (Big Cave) | Rimstone pools, huge chambers, barefoot scrambling | 2–3 hrs | Moderate | ₱800 per guide, up to ~4 people |
| Lumiang (entrance) | Burial cave with stacked coffins at the mouth | 30 min | Easy | Often included with the connection |
| Cave connection | Enter Lumiang, exit Sumaguing, with short rope descents | 3–4 hrs | Challenging | ₱1,500 per guide, small groups |

Rates change and differ by group size, so confirm them at the Tourist Information Center when you register.

## Sumaguing Cave: the classic

Sumaguing is the largest and most visited cave in Sagada, a short ride or a 30–40 minute walk south of town along the South Road. You climb down slippery stone steps past bat roosts into a cathedral-sized chamber, then follow your guide's lamp over smooth limestone. Lower down, you take off your shoes: the rock grips better barefoot, and you'll wade through cool rimstone pools and climb formations with names like the King's Curtain.

It's a proper adventure, but no ropes or experience are needed. Expect to get wet to the knees or waist and muddy everywhere. Most visitors take two to three hours.

## Lumiang Cave: the burial cave

Lumiang sits a short walk from the road, and its entrance holds more than a hundred wooden coffins stacked in the rock, some of them centuries old. For the Kankanaey people of Sagada, placing the dead in caves and on cliffs (like the hanging coffins of Echo Valley) is a living tradition, not a museum display.

You can see the coffins from the trail without going underground. Keep your voice low, don't touch or climb anything, and take photos respectfully, or not at all if you're asked not to.

## The cave connection: Lumiang to Sumaguing

The cave connection is Sagada's signature caving trip. You enter at Lumiang, descend through narrow passages and short drops on ropes held by your guide, squeeze through gaps, and finally climb out through Sumaguing's big chamber. It takes about three to four hours.

Choose it if you're fit, comfortable in tight spaces and happy to get soaked. Skip it if you're claustrophobic, have knee or back problems, or are traveling with young children: Sumaguing alone is still spectacular.

## Other caves near Sagada

- **Balangagan Cave:** farther from town and much quieter, with a longer approach walk. Ask at the Tourist Information Center whether it's open and guided when you visit.
- **Matangkib Cave:** a burial cave near Echo Valley, seen from outside on the hanging-coffins walk.
- **Crystal Cave:** closed to visitors for years to protect its formations. Don't trust anyone offering to take you in.

## How to book a cave tour

1. **Register first.** Every visitor registers and pays the environmental fee (≈ ₱100) at the Tourist Information Center in the town center.
2. **Book a guide there.** Guides are assigned by the accredited guide associations. Tell them which cave you want and how many people are in your group.
3. **Arrange transport.** Vans to Sumaguing cost about ₱350–400 per group, or you can walk.
4. **Go early.** Morning slots are quieter, and you'll be out before afternoon rain.

## What to wear and bring

- Clothes you don't mind ruining: quick-dry shorts or leggings and a T-shirt
- Sandals with straps (you'll go barefoot inside anyway)
- A change of clothes and a small towel, left in the van
- A waterproof pouch or dry bag for your phone
- Water and a snack

Leave jewelry and anything valuable at your inn. Guides carry lamps, but a headlamp keeps your hands free.

## Safety and respect

Caves flood after heavy rain, and guides will cancel or shorten trips when water levels rise, so don't argue. Follow your guide's footing exactly, never touch formations (skin oils stop them growing), and carry all trash out. Inside burial caves, the rule is simple: look, don't touch.

## Best time to go caving in Sagada

The dry months from November to May are the most comfortable, and March to May has the lowest water. Caves stay open in the rainy season, but trips are often cut short or cancelled from July to September. Pack a fleece either way: Sagada is cold at night, and you'll come out of the cave wet.
