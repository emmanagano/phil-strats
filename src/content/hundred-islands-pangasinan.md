Hundred Islands is a scatter of small, mushroom-shaped limestone islets in the Lingayen Gulf, just off Alaminos. A protected area since 1940, when it became Hundred Islands National Park, the area counts 124 islands at low tide, from Governor's Island with its viewpoint to Pilgrimage Island and its tall statue of Christ the Savior. It is one of the closest island-hopping destinations to Manila, which makes it a classic weekend escape.

### The main islands

Three islands have the most facilities. **Governor's Island** has a viewpoint over the whole park and the start of the zipline. **Quezon Island** is the busiest, with shelters, toilets and a swimming area. **Children's Island** has calm, shallow water for families. Other popular stops include Marcos Island, where visitors jump from the rocks into the water, and Pilgrimage Island, with its large statue of Christ the Savior and a hilltop viewpoint.

### Caves, clams and snorkeling

The islands are worn limestone, and many have small caves and overhangs carved by the sea. Boat tours often stop at Cuenco Cave and Imelda Cave. Around the islands, snorkeling spots and a giant clam sanctuary show the work done to restore marine life in the Lingayen Gulf. Visibility varies with the weather, so go on a calm, sunny morning.

### Pairing with Bolinao and Pangasinan

Hundred Islands fits in a weekend from Manila, but you can extend it with Bolinao's beaches, caves and lighthouse to the west, or stop in Lingayen and Dagupan for bangus (milkfish) and Pangasinan food on the way back.

### How island hopping works

Boats leave from Lucap Wharf in Alaminos between 6am and 5:30pm, and you pay per boat, not per person, so Hundred Islands is cheapest in a group.

1. Register at the tourism office and pay the day-tour fees, about ₱120–130 per person (environmental, entrance and coverage), plus a refundable trash deposit for the group.
2. Choose a boat by size: about ₱1,920 for the day for up to 5 people, ₱2,400 for up to 10, or ₱2,880 for up to 15. Four people sharing a small boat pay under ₱500 each.
3. Take a cheaper 3-island drop-off (from about ₱1,680) or keep the boat all day and pick your own stops.
4. Bring food and water. The islands have few or no shops.
